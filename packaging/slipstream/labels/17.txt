THE SHORTEST WAY
  1. Double-click  Install-Slipstream.cmd          10 to 20 minutes. Then play as you do now:
                                                   capture card, 4K, 30 FPS game, 2X or 3X.
     That is all. Nothing to edit, no test to run. ONE THING CHANGES THE PICTURE BY ITSELF: if
     Live HUD Lock is on (it is in your setup), the new crosshair protection (part A) runs with
     it. Everything else new either only measures and writes to the log, or is a switch that is
     off until you turn it on. Sections 2 and later of this file (the measuring kit) are optional.
  2. Look at the crosshair and the HUD while you turn the camera, at 2X and at 3X. That is the
     test of part A. If anything looks worse than Package 16, the undo line is at "TO UNDO".
  3. Optional: in quick setup switch "Steadier fine detail" on (part D) and judge thin lines, the
     crosshair and small text on your 1440p screen, in motion.
  NOTHING NEW IN THIS PACKAGE HAS RUN ON YOUR PC OR ON ANY REAL GRAPHICS CARD. Nothing is called
  fixed. No delay figure and no picture quality is promised: your eyes and your log decide.

WHAT PACKAGE 17 CHANGES   (capture card, 4K HDR10, 30 FPS shown at 2X or 3X; 1440p 180 Hz screen)
  How far each line is proven: SIMULATED = our own code run on synthetic pictures or made-up
  timings (no graphics card); TESTED = unit tests and the Windows build machine (a software
  renderer: it proves that the logic and the shaders agree with the reference, NOT speed, timing
  or how it looks); nothing new is HARDWARE-VERIFIED.
  A. Crosshair and HUD kept out of frame generation ("HUD Guard")   (ON with Live HUD Lock)
     - Why: an interpolator moves a 1 to 3 pixel crosshair with the background behind it, so a
       generated frame draws faded copies of it beside its place. Live HUD Lock alone put the
       opaque core back but left the copies.
     - What it does now, per real frame: it finds pixels that stay still while the picture
       around them moves (a camera turn), takes thin still shapes out of the picture NVIDIA's
       generator sees, and writes those pixels into every generated frame from the real frames.
       No frame waits for a later frame because of it.
     - New since the first draft of this part: a crosshair over a flat picture (sky, a wall,
       fog), where nothing around it moves enough to be noticed, is protected after it has been
       still for about 1 second, and only while nothing at all changes in the middle of the
       picture. SIMULATED only. On a real picture with film grain or noise that condition may
       never be met; then this addition does nothing and the rest works as described.
     - SIMULATED (synthetic pictures, a stand-in generator that moves the crosshair with the
       camera; a real generator may behave differently over a picture without texture): over a
       flat sky the crosshair's pixels were exact in every generated frame where the stand-in
       alone had about 12 percent; an object crossing the middle of a flat picture is not erased
       (0 wrong pixels with a perfect generator). TESTED: the shaders give the same result as
       that reference on the software renderer.
     - Known limits, found by the same tests and NOT fixed: an anti-aliased (soft-edged)
       crosshair's 1-pixel fringe is slightly worse than without the guard (about 3 of 255
       brightness steps) while its core is far better; over very bright HDR highlights about 87
       percent of the crosshair's pixels are exact, not all; see-through HUD parts (below about
       90 percent opacity) are not protected; protection starts about 200 ms after an overlay
       appears, so short hit markers never get it; after a crosshair disappears over a flat
       picture the pixels beside it stay marked for about a third of a second (harmless there).
     - Cost: its work is part of every real frame's GPU time, which every frame of the pair waits
       for. Estimated 0.25 to 0.6 ms at 4K; NOT MEASURED. Your log shows it once a second:
       [hud-guard] ... gpuHudGuardMs=   and whether it protects anything at the centre:
       aimProtectPx= .
  B. The settings say what is really running   (ON; display and log only)
     - Under the rows of quick setup and Professional mode a grey line now tells the difference
       between "you asked for it", "the engine accepted it" and "it was measured running in the
       last 10 seconds". Examples: "3X running, but only 1.6 generated frames on average after
       each real frame (some were skipped)"; "on; PushFPS has no counter that shows whether it is
       doing anything". Where nothing measures a setting it says so; it never says "running"
       from the setting alone. A "running now" block sums it up.
     - G-SYNC and NVIDIA low latency: the texts now say only what the driver reports. "NVIDIA
       low latency" here is a Reflex-style wait PushFPS asks the driver for, not the control
       panel's Low Latency Mode; with a capture card it acts on PushFPS's own part only and no
       effect was measured in your Package 16 log.
     - The same facts go to the log every 10 seconds and at every change: [settings-effect].
     - TESTED: the rules as unit tests, the texts as interface tests on the build machine.
  C. What reaches the screen, checked by the program itself   (ON; it measures and writes the log)
     - [cadence]: from Windows' own frame statistics, how evenly frames were actually shown,
       now also in milliseconds (showJitterMs, holdSdMs, judderMs), so 2X and 3X can be compared
       fairly: the older percentage made 3X look worse than 2X for the same display jitter.
     - [frame-probe]: after each shown frame a small thumbnail is compared with its neighbours to
       count repeated, blended and unevenly stepped pictures. Slow camera movement is no longer
       judged (it gave false alarms). SIMULATED and TESTED; the threshold was not tuned on a
       real game.
     - One notice, at most every 10 minutes: when PushFPS is full screen but another window has
       the focus and frames are shown unevenly, it asks you to click the picture. Switch
       "Remind me to click the picture in fullscreen" in quick setup turns it off.
     - None of these lines is a measurement at the display: only a camera or a light sensor is.
  D. "Steadier fine detail": the 4K picture shrunk to your 1440p screen by area   (OFF; a switch)
     - Today each screen pixel takes one sample of the 4K picture, so thin lines, the crosshair
       and small text flicker in brightness as they move. With the switch on, every 4K pixel
       counts by the area it covers, computed in linear light (correct for HDR10), without
       overshoot past the brightest pixel under it.
     - SIMULATED and TESTED (shader against reference, HDR10 and SDR). How sharp it looks and
       what it costs on your card are NOT measured: judge it by eye. Sharper variants exist for
       comparison (developers' line VEYRA_EXP_RP_SHRINK_KERNEL in slipstream.ini: area, sharp,
       clamp, catmull, catmull-clamp, lanczos, lanczos-clamp); none is chosen yet.
  E. 2X with duplicate removal   (ON)
     - With duplicate removal on, frame generation judged whether a pair was on time against the
       capture card's delivery instead of the moment its own schedule counts from, which could
       show a repeated frame at 2X. Both now use the same moment. SIMULATED; kept under test.
  F. "Lower delay": the start-up check of the motion estimation uses a better test picture
     (only matters if you switch "Lower delay" on; it stays OFF). SIMULATED and TESTED on the
     software renderer as far as that can run it; the estimator itself still needs a real card.
  NOT in this package: our own frame generator. It is in work, is never chosen automatically,
  and will come as a separate choice next to NVIDIA's.

WHAT TO EXPECT, HONESTLY
  - Delay: nothing here is meant to lower it. Part A adds a little GPU work per real frame
    (estimate above); if your delay readout rises by more than about 1 ms with Live HUD Lock on,
    that is a finding: please send the log.
  - A WARNING SIGN FROM THE BUILD MACHINE: in the engine test with your settings its software
    renderer no longer keeps up since this package (0.4 to 0.7 generated frames per real frame
    where Package 16 kept up; see section "what has been checked"). The cause is part A's
    extra work per real frame, which a software renderer does on the processor: with part A
    switched off the same test keeps up again. A graphics card should do that work in well
    under a millisecond, but nobody has measured it. If you see skipped generated frames, a
    multiplier that drops below 3X, or stutter that Package 16 did not have: add
    VEYRA_TEST_RP_LEGACY_HUD_GUARD = 1  (see TO UNDO), check again, and send the log either way.
  - Part A should remove most copies of a hard-edged crosshair in camera turns. It can also be
    wrong in ways the synthetic pictures did not show. You are the first to see it on a game.
  - Parts B and C change nothing in how frames are made, timed or shown (by reading the code and
    the build machine's tests; not measured on your PC).
  - The build machine's tests are no proof of timing, cost or stability on your card.

NEW IN PACKAGE 12 (also in this package)
  - Where frame generation's delay comes from: docs\FG_LATENCY.md. The three largest parts: the
    wait for the next real frame (inherent to any interpolation); a real frame held for the whole
    frame generation delay even when no generated frame is shown (avoidable); and Remote Play's
    fixed delay instead of a measured one (avoidable, about 4 ms at 60 -> 120 by arithmetic).
  - Low-latency frame generation, switch VEYRA_EXP_RP_FG_ULL = 1 (or only some parts: hold, phase,
    headroom, grace). It removes the two avoidable parts. It can only show frames earlier, never
    later; it may drop a few more generated frames, and the picture jumps forward once when a hold
    starts and stands still once when it ends. There is no menu entry yet: nothing is measured.
  - Predicting frames instead of waiting for the next one (extrapolation) was studied and not
    built: NVIDIA's frame generation only interpolates, and it could not beat frame generation off
    for responsiveness.
  - The "DLSS 5" stutter: docs\NR_STUTTER.md. The option is NVIDIA's own neural runtime run on the
    decoded video, not NVIDIA's DLSS 5 built into games. Most likely cause (from the code, the size
    is an estimate): when a frame's work takes longer than one frame of the game, decoded frames are
    replaced before they are used and late frames lose their generated frames. The four-way test of
    Package 12 measures it on your PC.
  - Switch VEYRA_EXP_RP_NR_ADAPT = 1: when time runs short, the neural layer reuses its last result
    on the next frames instead of running again. No buffer and no wait; its own motion pass adds a
    little GPU time to each real frame. Judge the picture by eye: a reused result is one frame old.
  - To try a switch in normal play: put its line in a file named slipstream.ini next to the
    experimental copy's PushFPS.exe (%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\PushFPS-slipstream-exp), one switch
    per line, then restart it. Delete the line to switch it off. The test kit ignores this file.

----- the install question (section 1) -----
   THE ONE QUESTION: the neural layer needs NVIDIA's own runtime file. The installer keeps it only
   when you say yes, and only when the file is an NVIDIA-signed original; it never installs a
   modified file. Without it everything else works, and the neural arms of the kit say
   "not installed: skipped". To answer in advance:
       Install-Slipstream.cmd -NeuralRuntime Yes       (or No; Ask is the default)
   UPDATING an earlier experimental copy (Package 7, 8A or 8B): an NVIDIA runtime copy that came with
   an earlier package without your yes is removed at this update, and a layer saved on it becomes
   unavailable. Answering no removes every copy, also one you added in Settings. Answer yes, or add
   it later in that copy's Settings, Components and licenses.
   THE OTHER THREE COPIES (original, stable, Package 7) use their older installers: on an NVIDIA PC
   they keep the NVIDIA-signed runtime file from Veyra's package without asking, as PushFPS 3.2.1
   does. Their neural layer still stays off until you turn it on.
